{\Large\bfseries Inputs for the neutron--proton converter}

This package contains \field{npc_prebreakout_models.h5}. It has six
pre-breakout jet/ejecta calculations: \field{ana_reference},
\field{ana_neutron_rich}, \field{ana_steeper_tail}, \field{num_dd2_equal},
\field{num_sfho}, and \field{num_sly}. The time coordinate is in the lab
frame. Each model has 261 samples (attribute \field{sampling}): 201 uniform
in time from the launch $t_0$ to $0.99$ of the propagation, plus 60 with
$(t_{\rm bo}-t)/(t_{\rm bo}-t_0)$ geometric from $0.3$ to $10^{-4}$, merged.
The last sample lies at $10^{-4}$ of the propagation before breakout.

\begin{sloppypar}
The HDF5 file is self-describing: every dataset has \field{unit} and
\field{frame} attributes. The file schema is
\field{jetbns.npc-model-export.v4} (per-model \field{jetbns.npc-inputs.v5}).
\end{sloppypar}

\section*{Reading a model}

Only Python, NumPy, and h5py are needed:

\begin{verbatim}
import h5py

with h5py.File("npc_prebreakout_models.h5", "r") as f:
    g = f["models/ana_reference"]
    time = g["time_s"][:]
    radius = g["radius_cm"][:]
    Gamma_rel = g["relative_lorentz_factor"][:]
    n_p = g["proton_number_density_cm3"][:]
    n_n = g["free_neutron_number_density_cm3"][:]
    B = g["upstream_magnetic_field_g"][:]
    tau_np = g["neutron_to_proton_optical_depth"][:]
    tau_pn = g["proton_to_neutron_optical_depth"][:]
    col_lc = g["exterior_pn_column_lightcone"][:]

    t_bo = g.attrs["breakout_time_s"]
    r_bo = g.attrs["breakout_radius_cm"]
\end{verbatim}

The last array element is the last pre-breakout sample; it is not the exact
breakout state. The exact breakout time and radius are stored as group
attributes. The group \field{prebreakout_summary} contains the corresponding
endpoint values for all models.

Datasets that carry a convention also have a \field{role} attribute. The
group \field{jet_cocoon} contains the cocoon radius, energy, pressure,
deposition rate, jet area, and jet opening angle. The group
\field{snapshot_columns} keeps the legacy frozen-slice columns at the
$0.99$ sample; they use the factor $\Gamma_a(1-\beta_a)$ on a frozen profile,
undercount the light-cone column by $1.8$--$5.3$, and are superseded by
\field{exterior_pn_column_lightcone}.

\section*{Exterior columns}

The conversion depth for the Monte Carlo is the all-baryon $pn$ column met by
a particle that leaves the jet head at $(r_h,t)$ and moves radially outward at
$c$,
\[
\tau_{\rm lc}=\int_{r_h}^{r_{\rm exit}}\frac{\rho(r,t_r)}{m_p}\,\sigma_{pn}\,
\Gamma_a(1-\beta_a)\,dr, \qquad t_r=t+\frac{r-r_h}{c},
\]
with $\rho$ the proper density, $\beta_a$ the lab-frame ejecta speed at
$(r,t_r)$, and $r_{\rm exit}$ the radius where the light cone leaves the
ejecta (\field{lightcone_exit_radius_cm}). If it has not left them at
$100\,r_h$ the integral stops there and \field{lightcone_exited} is 0. For a
steady wind $\tau_{\rm lc}=n_u\sigma_{pn}r/(2\Gamma_a)$. The grid has 512
points geometric in $r-r_h$. Two frozen-slice integrals at lab time $t$, from
$r_h$ to the outer ejecta radius (1536 geometric points), are given for
reference:
\[
\tau_{\rm static}=\int\frac{\rho}{m_p}\sigma_{pn}\,dr, \qquad
\tau_{\rm grey}=\int\kappa\rho\,dr,\quad \kappa=0.16~{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}.
\]
$\tau_{\rm grey}$ is the depth of the breakout criterion
$\tau_{\rm grey}=1/\beta'_s$; it is close to 1 at the last sample. The legacy
local depth \field{pn_optical_depth} $=n_b\sigma_{pn}r/\Gamma_a$ is
30--120 times $\tau_{\rm lc}$ near breakout.

\section*{Downstream radiation state}

\begin{sloppypar}
\field{downstream_temperature_thermalised_kev} is $k_BT$ for a fully
thermalised downstream, $aT^4=e_d$ with the jump-condition energy density
$e_d=(\Gamma_{\rm rel}-1)(4\Gamma_{\rm rel}+3)\rho_uc^2$ (adiabatic index
$4/3$); it is $\simeq4^{1/4}$ times the legacy
\field{downstream_temperature_kev} and a lower limit on the immediate
downstream temperature.
\field{photon_rich_threshold_photons_per_baryon} $=(m_p/m_e)\gamma_u$, with
$\gamma_u$ the shock Lorentz factor in the upstream frame, is the upstream
photon-to-baryon ratio above which the shock is photon-rich (Ito et al.
2018, 2020); \field{pair_loading_estimate} $=100\gamma_u$ is the number of
pairs per baryon in the immediate downstream of a photon-starved shock
(Budnik et al. 2010). The file attribute \field{radiation_state_note} states
the convention: the jet-head shock in merger ejecta is photon-starved, and the
immediate downstream is expected at 100--200 keV with pair loading
(\field{pair_regulated_temperature_kev}).
\end{sloppypar}

\section*{Density and optical-depth convention}

The exported free-nucleon estimates are
\[
n_p=Y_e\rho/m_p, \qquad n_n=X_{n,\rm free}\rho/m_p.
\]
The two local encounter depths use the same $pn$ cross section and the
effective upstream path length:
\[
\tau_{n:p}=n_p\sigma_{pn}\ell_u, \qquad
\tau_{p:n}=n_n\sigma_{pn}\ell_u.
\]
The notation is projectile:target. These are local input diagnostics, not
full transport probabilities or spectra. The neutron fraction is a
phenomenological free-neutron estimate; bound nuclei and conversion branching
are left to the transport calculation.

The archive is intended to be used as-is. It contains only this note and the
HDF5 file.
